- PDF viewer: scanned pages can now be read. When the page in view is an image without a text layer (or the whole PDF is a scan), the viewer offers **Recognize text**. Text recognition (OCR) is built in and runs on this computer only: the Tesseract engine and its English data ship inside the app, nothing is downloaded and nothing is uploaded. It reads the pages without text one after the other, starting with the page in view, shows its progress and can be stopped at any time; pages already recognized stay recognized.
- Recognized words are always marked as such: each word has a dotted violet frame (fainter for uncertain readings) and shows its confidence on hover, the page carries a "Recognized text" badge with the average confidence, and a notice sums up the recognized pages and words. They are searchable in the viewer and in the unified search, and a recognized word becomes a link to the board only when it is exactly a board reference or net name and was read with a confidence of at least 60 %. A misread name (for example "CL" for C1) is simply not linked; links from recognized text are drawn dotted and their label says "recognized text" and the confidence.
- Recognition runs in a separate worker with no network access and does not change the page's security policy. A page gets two passes (as displayed, and turned a quarter for vertical labels), at most 40 megapixels and a time budget of two minutes; a page over the budget is reported and the next page continues. Results are kept for the session: the same file opened again shows its recognized text at once.
- Package: the OCR engine (two WebAssembly builds) and the English data add about 8.9 MB to the application. Their licences (Apache-2.0 for Tesseract and its data; Leptonica, libpng, zlib, IJG libjpeg, LibTIFF and libwebp, which are compiled into the engine) are listed in THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md.
